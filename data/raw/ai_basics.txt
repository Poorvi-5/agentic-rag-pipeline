Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Basics

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to the simulation of human intelligence in machines 
that are programmed to think and learn. AI systems can perform tasks that typically 
require human intelligence such as visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making, 
and language translation.

Machine Learning is a subset of AI that enables systems to learn and improve from 
experience without being explicitly programmed. Machine learning focuses on developing 
computer programs that can access data and use it to learn for themselves.

Deep Learning is a subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers. 
These deep neural networks are capable of learning representations of data with multiple 
levels of abstraction.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI systems trained on massive amounts of text data. 
They can generate human-like text, answer questions, summarize documents, and perform 
many other language tasks. Examples include GPT-4, Claude, and Llama.

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines information retrieval 
with text generation. Instead of relying solely on the LLM's training data, RAG systems 
first retrieve relevant documents from a knowledge base, then use that context to generate 
accurate and grounded responses.

Vector databases store data as mathematical vectors — arrays of numbers that represent 
the meaning of text. FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) is a popular vector database 
for efficient similarity search. ChromaDB is another vector database that offers 
persistent storage and easy integration with LangChain.

Embeddings are numerical representations of text that capture semantic meaning. 
Similar texts have similar embeddings. The sentence-transformers library provides 
pre-trained models to generate high-quality embeddings locally without any API calls.
